Apartment moving cost calculator: what to include

A move is not just rent. Most renters need cash for the first month, deposit, movers, supplies, utility setup, insurance and first-week essentials.

Use this guide with the free MoveBudget calculator.

1. Upfront housing costs

Start with security deposit, first month rent, application fees, admin fees, pet fees and any required building move-in fees.

2. Physical move costs

Add movers, truck rental, gas, tolls, parking, elevator reservations, packing supplies and tips. These costs vary by distance and building rules.

3. Setup and replacement costs

Include utilities, internet, renters insurance, cleaning supplies, furniture, kitchen basics and emergency cash for first-week surprises.

Checklist before you sign

  • Confirm refundable vs non-refundable fees.
  • Ask the building about elevator, parking or move-in fees.
  • Get mover quotes in writing.
  • Keep a buffer for setup costs after the move.

FAQ

What's the single most forgotten moving cost?

Building-specific fees: elevator reservation deposits, move-in fees, or parking permits for the moving truck. These aren't part of rent or the mover's quote, so they get missed until move-in week.

Should I budget for a full furniture replacement?

Only the essentials for the first week — bed, basic seating, kitchen items. Spread larger furniture purchases over the following months rather than buying everything before move-in, unless the budget genuinely allows it.

How much buffer should be added for first-week surprises?

A common approach is adding 10-15% on top of the itemized total specifically for first-week surprises — a missing cleaning supply, an extra parking fee, a forgotten adapter — rather than assuming the itemized list is complete.
