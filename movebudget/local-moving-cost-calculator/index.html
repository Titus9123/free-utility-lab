DIY vs hiring local movers

A local move (typically under 50 miles) is where DIY genuinely competes with hiring movers, unlike a long-distance move where professional logistics usually win. Compare a truck rental plus your own time and labor against a flat local-mover quote — for a studio or one-bedroom, DIY often wins on cost; for a multi-bedroom home, the labor savings from movers frequently offsets their price.

Costs specific to local moves

  • Truck rental by the day or by mileage, whichever the rental company charges for a short-distance move
  • Fuel and tolls — usually minor for a local move but still worth including
  • Parking permits at both the old and new address on move day
  • Short-term storage if the move dates don't line up exactly

FAQ

Is DIY always cheaper for a local move?

Not always — it depends on how much you own and how much your time is worth. For a small apartment, DIY usually wins; for a larger home, professional movers can offset their cost through speed and reduced risk of damage.

Do local moves need the same insurance as long-distance ones?

Requirements vary by mover and state — ask any company you hire what coverage is included and whether additional valuation coverage is available, even for a short-distance move.
