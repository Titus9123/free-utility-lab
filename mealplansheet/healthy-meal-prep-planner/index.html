What to include

  • Simple categories that match how you actually plan.
  • A buffer for forgotten items, changes or uncertainty.
  • A printable or reusable output when possible.

Common mistake

A structure that works without a named diet

Build each prepped meal from three parts: a protein (chicken, beans, eggs, tofu), a vegetable (roasted, steamed or raw), and a starch (rice, potato, whole grain). Cook each part in a batch, then mix and match across the week instead of prepping five identical, fully-assembled meals — it prevents the boredom that ends most meal prep attempts by week two.

FAQ

What makes a meal prep plan 'healthy' without following a specific diet?

Consistently including a protein source, a vegetable, and a whole-grain or starch in each prepped meal — that structure works across most eating styles without needing to follow a named diet plan.

How long do prepped meals last in the fridge?

Most cooked prepped meals are commonly kept 3-4 days refrigerated; freeze portions you won't eat within that window rather than pushing the fridge timeline. Always use your own judgment and food safety guidance for specific ingredients.

Should all meals be prepped on one day?

Not necessarily. Splitting prep across two shorter sessions (e.g. proteins one day, vegetables and grains another) is often more sustainable than one long Sunday session, especially for a first attempt at meal prep.
