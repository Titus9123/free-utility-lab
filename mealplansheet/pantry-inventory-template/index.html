Three lists, not one

Split the inventory into shelf-stable pantry, fridge, and freezer — each has a different check frequency and a different risk of duplicate buying. Freezer items are the easiest to forget entirely since they're out of sight; a short standalone freezer list prevents buying a second bag of something already frozen at the back.

Check before you shop, not after

Glance at the inventory while writing the grocery list, not while unpacking groceries at home — that's the only point in the cycle where it actually prevents a duplicate purchase. Keep the list somewhere you already open before a shopping trip, like the notes app you use for the grocery list itself.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a pantry inventory be redone?

A quick check before each grocery trip (weekly for most households) catches near-duplicate purchases; a fuller inventory with expiration checks once a month or once a season catches items that get forgotten in the back of a shelf.

Should the freezer be inventoried separately from the pantry?

Yes — freezer items get forgotten even more easily than shelf-stable pantry items since they're less visible. A short separate freezer list prevents buying a duplicate of something already frozen.

What's the point of a pantry inventory if I'm just going to forget to check it?

Keep it somewhere you already look before shopping — the fridge door or the notes app you open to write the grocery list — so checking it becomes part of an existing habit rather than an extra step to remember.
