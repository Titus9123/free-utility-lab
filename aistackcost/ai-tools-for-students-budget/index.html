What a student AI budget should cover

Coursework needs two things most: a general assistant for explaining concepts and drafting, and a research tool that shows its sources so citations hold up. Video, image and automation tools that creators or agencies need rarely apply to student work, so skip them unless a specific class project calls for one.

General assistant: ChatGPT or Claude — free tiers cover most coursework; paid ~$20/mo only if you hit daily usage limits (openai.com / anthropic.com).
Cited research: Perplexity — free tier for occasional lookups, Pro ~$20/mo for heavy research terms (perplexity.ai).
Notes & study planning: Notion — free for individual students, no paid upgrade needed for personal use (notion.com).

Before you use AI on graded work

Academic integrity policies on AI use vary by school, department and even individual instructor — some allow AI for brainstorming but not drafting, others ban it outright for certain assignment types. Check your syllabus or ask directly before using any AI tool on graded coursework; this matters far more than which specific tool you choose.

Frequently asked questions

Do students get discounts on AI tools?

Some vendors offer education pricing or promotions from time to time (for example on Perplexity Pro or Canva Pro). Availability and terms change often and vary by school and region, so check each vendor's education page directly rather than assuming a discount applies.

Is it against academic integrity rules to use AI for schoolwork?

It depends entirely on your school's and instructor's policy, which varies widely. Always check your syllabus or ask your instructor before using AI for research, drafting or citations on graded work.

What's the cheapest useful student AI setup?

Free tiers of a general assistant plus a citation-aware research tool cover most coursework. Paying for anything is rarely necessary until you have heavy, daily research or writing volume.

Are prices guaranteed?

No. Prices and student promotions change often — verify current terms on each vendor's own pricing or education page.
