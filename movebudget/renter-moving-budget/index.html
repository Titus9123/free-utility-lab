What renters specifically need beyond "moving costs"

A generic moving budget stops at movers and boxes. A renter also owes the landlord money before the move even starts — deposit, first month, sometimes last month — and owes utility and internet providers setup fees the same week. Track landlord-owed costs, provider-owed costs, and physical-move costs as three separate groups; combining them into one number hides which ones have hard deadlines.

Deadlines matter more than totals here

Deposit and first month are typically due before or at lease signing — before the physical move happens at all. Line up cash availability against dates, not just against the total: having enough money by the end of the month doesn't help if the deposit is due two weeks earlier.

FAQ

What renter-specific costs get missed most often?

Utility and internet setup/activation fees, and any required renters insurance minimum — these arrive as separate provider charges, not part of the lease or the mover's bill.

Should first month and deposit be budgeted as one line item?

Keep them separate even though they're often paid together — the deposit is refundable (in principle) at move-out, while first month rent is not, which matters when estimating what cash actually comes back later.
