What a full estimate actually includes

A subscription cost calculator is only useful if it counts every recurring AI charge, not just the one you're thinking about adding. That means: every general assistant seat, every design or writing tool, every automation plan, and any per-user add-on billed monthly or annually. Most people underestimate their real total by 2-3x because they mentally price tools one at a time instead of adding the full list.

A repeatable monthly review

List every active AI tool once, mark who actually uses each one weekly, multiply price by seats, then add annual plans divided by 12. Cancel anything nobody used in the last 30 days before adding anything new — that single habit catches most of the overspend a one-time budget estimate misses.

FAQ

What should I include in an AI subscription cost estimate?

Every paid seat across every tool, not just one subscription: general assistants, design tools, automation platforms, and any per-user add-ons, each multiplied by how many people actually have a seat.

Why does the real total always look bigger than expected?

Because subscriptions are usually approved one at a time, so no single decision ever feels expensive. Add every active tool to one list and the combined monthly total is often 2-3x what any one person estimated from memory.

How often should the total be reviewed?

Monthly for a small team, quarterly at minimum. Renewals for annual plans are the most common source of surprise cost, since nobody re-evaluates them until the charge appears.

Does this calculator store or send my numbers anywhere?

No. The calculator runs in your browser only; nothing you type is uploaded, and no signup is required.
