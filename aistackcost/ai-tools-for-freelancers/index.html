A lean freelancer AI stack

A solo freelancer rarely needs more than three paid tools: one general assistant for writing, research and client communication, one production tool matched to the actual deliverable (design, code, video or audio), and one lightweight workspace for notes and invoices. Everything beyond that is usually redundant capability you're paying for twice.

How to decide what to pay for

Track two weeks of real work first. If a free tier's usage cap gets hit more than once or twice, or a missing feature costs you rework time, that's the signal to upgrade — not marketing copy or a colleague's recommendation. Cancel a subscription the same week you notice you haven't opened it; freelancers lose more to forgotten renewals than to any single overpriced tool.

FAQ

How much should a freelancer budget for AI tools per month?

Most solo freelancers do fine on $20-40/month: one general assistant (ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, roughly $20/month each) plus one production tool such as Canva. Add a third paid tool only once a specific weekly task justifies it.

Should a freelancer pay for every major AI assistant?

Usually no. Start with one primary assistant and add a second only for a specific workflow advantage, such as one model's stronger long-document handling or coding support.

Are free AI tools enough before paying for anything?

Often yes for the first few months. Free tiers of ChatGPT, Gemini and Canva cover light, occasional use; upgrade only once usage limits or missing features actually block paid client work.

Are these AI prices guaranteed?

No. Prices and features change often. Treat every monthly cost on this page as an approximate planning figure and check the official pricing page before buying.
